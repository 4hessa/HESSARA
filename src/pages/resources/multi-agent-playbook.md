A multi-agent consultation is a structured argument between specialist advisors, run for the benefit of a person who has to decide. This playbook sets out the roles, the sequence, and the points at which a person should step in.

## When to use it

Use a consultation for decisions with real tension: competing goals, uncertain outcomes, or stakeholders who disagree. For routine analysis, a single advisor is quicker and just as good. The reasoning behind this is set out in [How multi-agent deliberation changes the shape of a strategy review](/insights/multi-agent-deliberation/).

## Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Convenor (you) | Owns the question, the constraints, and the final decision |
| Executive Strategist | Proposes options and argues for a preferred course |
| Risk & Foresight Oracle | Challenges each option and names early warning signs |
| Systems Architect | Maps dependencies and second-order effects |

## The sequence

### 1. Brief

Give every advisor the same one-sentence question, the constraints, and the same documents. Record the brief; you will want it later.

### 2. Independent positions

Ask each advisor for a position before they see the others. This is the step most often skipped, and the one that makes later agreement meaningful.

### 3. Challenge

Have each advisor respond to the others. Ask specifically: what would have to be true for the opposing view to be right?

### 4. Simulation

Where the disagreement turns on an uncertain outcome, test it. Set out two or three scenarios and ask how each option performs under each. See the [scenario simulation briefing](/insights/scenario-simulation/).

### 5. Synthesis

Ask for a summary that keeps the disagreement visible: where the advisors agree, where they differ, and what evidence would settle it.

### 6. Decision

The convenor decides. Record the decision, the reasons, and the conditions under which you would revisit it.

## Review points

Stop and read carefully at three moments:

- **After the brief:** is this the real question?
- **After independent positions:** do the advisors disagree for substantive reasons?
- **Before the decision:** have you checked the sources behind the claims you are relying on?

## Signs it is going wrong

- All advisors agree at once, on the first pass
- The discussion drifts from the question in the brief
- Claims appear without sources
- The synthesis reads as a compromise that no advisor argued for
