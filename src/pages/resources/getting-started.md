A first session goes well when the preparation is done before the conversation begins. This guide walks through the four stages: prepare, frame, convene, and review.

## 1. Prepare your material

Collect the documents the question depends on: reports, plans, figures, and prior decisions. Fewer, more relevant documents produce better answers than a large unsorted archive. Remove anything you would not be comfortable having quoted back to colleagues.

## 2. Frame the question

Write the question as a single sentence, then add:

- **The decision it serves** and when that decision is due
- **Constraints** that are not open for discussion
- **What a useful answer looks like:** a recommendation, a comparison, a list of risks

A vague question produces a general answer. "Should we expand?" is weaker than "Which of these two markets should we enter first, given a fixed budget and an eighteen-month horizon?"

## 3. Convene the right specialists

Choose advisors whose perspectives differ. The [AI Consulting](/consulting/) page describes the three council roles:

| Role | Brings |
| --- | --- |
| Executive Strategist | Options, trade-offs, and sequencing |
| Risk & Foresight Oracle | What could go wrong, and early signals |
| Systems Architect | How the parts depend on one another |

One specialist is enough for a narrow question. Use a full council when the decision has real tension in it. See the [multi-agent playbook](/resources/multi-agent-playbook/) for a fuller method.

## 4. Review the reasoning

Read the answer as you would read a colleague's draft:

1. Check that each claim points to a source you recognise.
2. Separate what the sources say from what was inferred.
3. Note what is missing, and ask a follow-up question.
4. Record the decision you take and why.

## Common first-session mistakes

- Asking several questions at once
- Supplying no documents and expecting specific answers
- Treating the first answer as final instead of as the start of a conversation
